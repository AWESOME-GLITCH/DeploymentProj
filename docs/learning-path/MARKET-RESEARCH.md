No public cohort retention data exists for Speak, ELSA or Praktika; their figures are vendor-reported.

Implications for Learning Path:

- Dropping hearts and leagues is now the mainstream direction, not a contrarian bet
- Make the coach message a growth-mindset coach and add an if-then plan at onboarding; these carry the strongest measured adult retention effects
- AI speaking must be free from day one; at under a cent a call it no longer differentiates, so AZE scoring and ES teachers must
- Design explicitly for over-40s: large type, three-step onboarding, delayed registration, a passive buddy link to a real cohort
- Measure session depth (atoms completed, minutes of speech produced), not only daily return, and offer a one-month free trial rather than seven days

## Q3 and Q4: efficacy evidence and AI scoring fairness

No competitor has a controlled, externally scored trial with effect sizes, and speech recognition still mis-transcribes L2 speakers two to six times more often than natives. Both are open ground for Learning Path.

| Product | Study | Design | Claim | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Duolingo | Jiang, Peters, Plonsky and Pajak 2024, CALICO Journal ([paper](https://cdnprodwpv2.avantassessment.com/wp-content/uploads/Duolingo-English-Courses-in-Developing-Proficiency.pdf)) | Single-group, post-test only, n = 245, STAMP reading and listening; three of four authors Duolingo staff | End of A2 content yields early B1 receptive scores; about 122 hours to end of A2 | No pre-test, no control, no effect sizes, self-reported hours, receptive skills only |
| Duolingo | Jiang et al., Foreign Language Annals ([summary](https://www.goodnewsnetwork.org/study-shows-duolingo-learning-outcomes-are-comparable-to-university-classes/)) | Cross-sectional comparison with university students | Five units equal four semesters on reading and listening | Vendor-authored, no randomisation, no speaking or writing |
| Duolingo | Smith, Jiang and Peters 2024, [Language Learning and Technology](https://www.lltjournal.org/item-detail/1152/) | Three-month pre and post, n = 48 | Significant gains after about 27 hours | Small sample, no control, vendor co-authored |
| Babbel | Vesselinov and Grego 2016 ([press release](https://www.babbel.com/press/en-us/releases/2016-09-29-Efficacy_Study.html)) | Two-month pre and post, 391 enrolled, WebCAPE | 15 hours covers one college semester for novices | Commissioned, no control, attrition unreported |
| Babbel | Loewen et al. 2019 ([MSU](https://msutoday.msu.edu/news/2019/new-study-gives-insight-on-effectiveness-of-language-learning-apps)) | Quasi-experimental 12 weeks, 85 start, 54 finish, OPI | 59% gained at least one ACTFL sub-level; 75% among those with 15+ hours | 36% attrition, no control |
| Busuu | Vesselinov and Grego 2016 ([Busuu](https://www.busuu.com/en/research/university-study)) | Eight weeks pre and post, n = 196 | 22 hours Premium equals one college semester | Only heavy users sat exit tests, commissioned |
| Speak | None found ([Speak.com](https://www.speak.com/) cites downloads and ratings) | Not applicable | Proprietary loop | No published efficacy data (estimate: none as of October 2026) |
| ELSA | Small classroom studies only, e.g. [Hoeriyah 2024](https://repository.uinsaizu.ac.id/27586) | Quasi-experiment, Indonesian secondary pupils | 79.2 versus 74.3 on pronunciation | Not peer reviewed, sample and duration unreported |

Pattern: every vendor claim rests on single-group or post-only designs, vendor authorship, and semester equivalence derived from course requirements rather than measured CEFR movement.

A stronger design for Learning Path: a pre-registered randomised wait-list or active-control trial (app with the spacing engine versus without, or versus ES World classroom hours) with intention-to-treat analysis and reported attrition; outcomes on an external test not built by AZE alongside AZE checkpoints, with blinded human raters on speaking and writing; logged rather than self-reported study time so hours-to-level is observed per CEFR band and L1; standardised effect sizes with confidence intervals, subgroups by age band and L1, six-month retention; an independent analyst, open data and a published protocol before collection.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Duolingo English Test | Feature-based ML on expert-rated samples, proctors review flagged responses ([DET scoring](https://blog.englishtest.duolingo.com/how-is-the-duolingo-english-test-scored/)) | States review across L1, gender and country; item-level DIF published, no scorer-level subgroup numbers public ([fairness page](https://blog.englishtest.duolingo.com/fairness)) | Asynchronous, results in days, not an API |
| Cambridge (ALTA, ELiT) | Whisper-based ASR plus holistic scoring and spoken grammatical error correction; open 340-hour Speak and Improve 2025 corpus, A2 to C1 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.11986v1)) | Globally sourced corpus, no per-L1 breakdown yet; challenge built to benchmark fairness openly | Research pipeline, non-commercial licence |
| Stanford and Cambridge (Roll and Graham) | Benchmarked ten ASR models on 300+ English varieties ([SLaTE 2025](https://www.isca-archive.org/slate_2025/roll25_slate.pdf)) | L2 word error rate 2.6 to 5.9 times L1; gap widens as models scale; tonal-L1 speakers about 10% WER versus 5.5% for stress-accent L1s | Bigger models cost more and do not close the gap |

| SpeechAce | Pronunciation and fluency API, claims accent alone does not lower scores ([FAQ](https://api-docs.speechace.com/other-resources/faqs)) | Claims third-party validation, figures on request | US$0.008 to 0.0125 per 15 s of speech; US$0.048 per 400 characters of writing |
| Pearson Versant, ETS SpeechRater, ELSA | Established feature-based scorers and proprietary accent-feedback ASR | No 2025 to 2026 public subgroup fairness reports located (estimate); British Council guidance asks vendors to disclose reliability by L1 and age ([Breakspear](https://www.britishcouncil.cn/sites/default/files/considerations_in_the_development_and_validation_of_automated_speaking_assessment_final.pdf)) | Versant scores in minutes; institutional pricing |

Implications for Learning Path:

- A pre-registered RCT with blinded raters and intention-to-treat reporting would be a category-first evidence claim; ES World classrooms are a natural active control
- Replace "hours equal a semester" with observed hours-to-CEFR-band per L1 and age group, confirmed by AZE checkpoints and one external test
- Benchmark the AZE engine per L1 (Arabic, Hindi and Urdu, Mandarin, Spanish, Portuguese) against the Speak and Improve corpus and publish subgroup agreement, which no commercial rival does
- Prioritise audio capture quality in the mobile UX (noise gating, re-record prompts): recording conditions shifted error rates more than accent did
- Budget scoring at about US$0.01 per 15 s of speech and US$0.05 per 400 characters of writing as the ceiling; a self-hosted Whisper variant for practice and the AZE engine for checkpoints keeps cost low while reserving the validated scorer for mastery decisions

## Q5 and Q6: mediation and interaction, buyer requirements, and video formats

No app teaches mediation as a strand; it lives in coursebooks, teacher guides and state exams. Institutional buyers treat CEFR alignment as a threshold and then score on reliability, audit, data protection, accessibility, analytics and human interaction. On video, the evidence favours short, presenter-visible, brisk, L2-captioned atoms, with AI avatars acceptable for scale.

Implications for Learning Path:

- Mediation is an open space; build atoms around summarise, relay, simplify and explain a chart, mapped to Companion Volume descriptors; Spain's EOI system is a ready B2B2C market
- Tender-proof the product: document reliability evidence, audit trail, GDPR, WCAG and neurodiversity accessibility, SCORM or LTI export and progress analytics from day one
- Plan for Eaquals-style scrutiny: scheduled human tutor touchpoints and learner-to-learner interaction, or many university buyers will reject a pure self-study app
- Format: one point per atom, presenter visible, informal set, brisk speech with a slow toggle below A2, L2 captions by default with tap-to-gloss L1
- AI avatars for long-tail and L1-localised atoms; human voice and real teachers for hero content; spend the saving on visual design

| Mexico | New LFPDPPP in force 21 Mar 2025, no statutory age threshold ([White and Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/mexico-enacts-new-data-protection-regime)) | Best interests of the child principle; extra care on advertising to minors ([Chambers](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/mexico/trends-and-developments)) | Not stated |

Implications for Learning Path:

- Price the consumer tier at US$6 to 9 a month annualised, with regional pricing; anchor against Busuu and Super Duolingo, not Max
- Keep core lessons free with a daily cap; paywall AI speaking depth, certificates and offline, the split most peers have converged on
- Quote B2B at US$9 to 18 per user per month with volume tiers, plus a per-hour pool for ES World live tutoring, so institutions compare like for like with Busuu, ELSA and Preply
- One global teen mode for 16 to 17s: profiling and ads off, streak and push nudges optional, parent-email gate below the local consent age; this satisfies the UK Code, DSA guidelines and the UAE law in one design
- India and Brazil need real age assurance and verifiable parental consent, not a checkbox; plan DigiLocker and app-store age-signal integrations before May 2027, and route school-bundled students through the institution as the consenting party where the exemption allows

## Innovation opportunities, ranked

The research confirms the PRD's core bets and reorders their emphasis: evidence and mediation are the widest open spaces, AI speaking on its own is no longer a differentiator, and the human school is worth more than the PRD gave it credit for. Ranked by how open the space is and how much it would move adoption by learners and institutions.

| Rank | Opportunity | Why the space is open | What it means for the PRD |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Category-first evidence: a pre-registered RCT with blinded raters, external test, hours-to-CEFR-band per L1 and age | Every competitor claim is single-group, vendor-authored and semester-equivalence based | Promote the efficacy plan from a metrics footnote to a launch deliverable; budget an independent analyst and external test in v1 |
| 2 | Mediation and interaction as taught, scored strands | No app does it; Spain's EOI exams weight mediation 20%; ministries and publishers only have teacher-led material | Keep both strands at launch; add Spain and other CV-aligned exam systems to go-to-market; publish the descriptor mapping openly |
| 3 | Published per-L1 fairness for speech scoring | L2 word error rates are 2.6 to 5.9 times L1 and no commercial vendor publishes subgroup agreement | Benchmark the AZE engine against the Speak and Improve corpus and publish results; make audio capture quality a P0 UX requirement |
| 4 | Human presence without human contact | Lack of human contact is the top dropout reason for older learners; Eaquals and university buyers require tutor touchpoints | Product decision: fully online, no tutoring or staff contact in the app. Meet the evidence with the automated supporter sentence (R34), the pod and peer voice notes (R23), peer presence and vote-see-revote, and an ES World referral link only (R33) |
| 5 | Adult-first engagement design | Leaders are abandoning hearts and points; coaching messages (D14 +7.2%) and if-then plans (d = 0.36) beat badges | Add an if-then plan step to first run; make the coach message a growth-mindset coach; one-month free trial; study-buddy pairing |
| 6 | Adaptive video variants (how the lesson is told) | Competitors adapt which lesson comes next, not its density; avatar recall equals human so variants are cheap to produce | Keep R18 as a P1 and prototype in MVP with one level |
| 7 | Tender-ready from day one | Buyers score on reliability, audit, accessibility, SCORM or LTI, analytics; CEFR alignment is only the threshold | Add SCORM or LTI export, audit trail and WCAG conformance statement to P1; teen mode and DPIA to P0 |
| 8 | Regional pricing and a global teen mode | Peers cluster at US$6 to 10 a month and are converging on the same free-tier split; UAE, EU, India and Brazil now treat under-18s as children | Set regional pricing in R27; one teen mode satisfies all jurisdictions; plan DigiLocker and app-store age signals before May 2027 |

What the research did not support: AI conversation as a premium differentiator (now under a cent a call and in base tiers), points-style gamification, and semester-equivalence marketing claims. The PRD should drop any language that leans on these.

Suggested edits to the main tab: raise teacher escalation and tutor touchpoints to P1 (R33); add SCORM or LTI export and an audit trail as P1 requirements; add an if-then plan step and growth-mindset coach to R13 and the UX section; add per-L1 speech fairness benchmarking and audio capture quality to R6; set the free trial to one month in R27; and make the efficacy RCT a named v1 deliverable in Phasing.

## Learning innovation beyond apps: simple mechanisms with evidence

The deep research across classrooms, tutoring trials, habit science and behaviour megastudies points to one conclusion: the mechanisms with the largest replicated effects are plain (recall first, spaced echoes, a real task at the end of the week, forgiving re-entry, a plan made once), and the branded methods with charismatic founders have the weakest evidence. Learning Path is a fully online, self-serve product with no human tutoring or staff contact, so the human-contact findings below are either excluded or met by automated or peer equivalents.

| Mechanism | Evidence | Effect | Fits a 10-minute day? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Retrieval practice | Adesope et al. 2017, 272 effects ([summary](https://theeconomyofmeaning.com/2017/03/21/important-new-meta-analysis-on-the-testing-effect-with-some-surprises/)) | g = 0.61 overall; 0.82 when the gap is 1 to 6 days versus 0.56 same day | Yes: open every session with 2 minutes recalling yesterday and 3 to 6 days ago |
| Spaced practice in L2 | Kim and Webb 2022, 48 experiments ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/358406370_The_Effects_of_Spaced_Practice_on_Second_Language_Learning_A_Meta-Analysis)) | Medium to large; equal and expanding spacing equivalent; longer gaps win on delayed tests | Yes: keep the scheduler simple, favour longer gaps |
| Implementation intentions | Gollwitzer and Sheeran 2006 ([summary](https://goalsandprogress.com/implementation-intentions-research/)); newer 642-test synthesis smaller | d = 0.65 originally; about d = 0.27 to 0.36 in later syntheses | Yes: one 15-second "After I..., I will..." at onboarding and each Monday |
| Rewarding the return after a lapse | Milkman et al. 2021, 61,293 gym members, 54 interventions ([Nature](https://nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04128-4)) | Top intervention of 54; experts could not predict winners | Yes: welcome-back gift and a 3-minute warm restart; no streak reset |
| Habit formation | Lally et al. 2010 ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7188915/)) | Median 66 days to automaticity; missing one day did not materially affect it | Yes: one daily cue, same time; design for week 10 |
| Metacognition bookends | EEF Toolkit, +8 months, highest strand ([update](https://www.riseedumag.com/eef-teaching-and-learning-toolkit-updated/)) | Plan, monitor, evaluate inside challenging tasks | Yes: 20 seconds "Today I will" and "I can / not yet" |
| Feedback on task and strategy | EEF Toolkit ([toolkit](https://dera.ioe.ac.uk/id/eprint/33734/1/EEF-Teaching-Learning-Toolkit-October-2018.pdf)) | High impact; "what to change next" beats praise | Yes: AI feedback states the one thing to change |
| Task-based teaching | Bryfonski and McKay 2019 d = 0.93 ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322034328_TBLT_implementation_and_evaluation_A_meta-analysis)); re-estimated g = 0.61 ([IRAL](https://doi.org/10.1515/iral-2021-0218)) | Moderate, contested | Yes: the week ends in a real task, not a quiz |
| Extensive reading and listening | Jeon and Day 2016 ([Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/The-effectiveness-of-ER-on-reading-proficiency:-A-Jeon-Day/91e92465a118ac02ca67f6c7b043607a044ae53d)); incidental vocabulary meta-analysis ([Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-teaching/article/how-effective-is-second-language-incidental-vocabulary-learning-a-metaanalysis/E38E3468FD2090B1FA3051051DE8E70C)) | d = 0.57 (adjusted about 0.37); largest for adults and EFL; web stories beat paper; listening gains similar to reading | Yes: one graded 60 to 90 second story a day, no quiz attached |
| Explicit chunk teaching | Li and Lei 2024 collocation meta-analysis ([Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/13621688221131127)) | Explicit beats implicit | Yes: 5 to 8 chunks of the week recycled in every atom |
| Peer instruction (vote, see, revote) | Crouch and Mazur ([summary](https://derekbruff.org/2009/10/23/article-crouch-mazur-2001/); [PhysPort](https://www.physport.org/methods/section.cfm?G=Peer_Instruction&S=Research)) | Normalised gain 0.25 to 0.49, later 0.74 | Yes, asynchronously: answer, see anonymised peer answers and a model, answer again; no messaging |
| Specific, only-when-undone nudges | Georgia State Pounce RCT ([ERIC](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1194134.pdf)) | +3.3 points enrolment, 21% less summer melt | Yes: notifications only for a named unfinished step |
| Supporter sentence | Kraft and Rogers 2015 ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0272775715000497)); READY4K! ([JHR](https://jhr.uwpress.org/content/54/3/537)) | Credit failure 15.8% to 9.3%; child literacy +0.11 SD, +0.18 personalised | Yes, automated: the learner nominates a friend or family member who receives one sentence a week with the can-do goal and a question to ask in English; no staff involved |
| Weekly human call or tutor tier | Angrist et al. 2022 Botswana RCT ([Nature Human Behaviour](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-022-01381-z.pdf)); tutoring meta-analyses | +0.12 SD; SMS alone not significant; tutoring 0.29 to 0.37 SD, shrinking at scale | Excluded by product decision: fully online, no human contact |
| Branded methods (Pimsleur, Michel Thomas, Suggestopedia, TPRS, Dogme) | Vendor-funded or small studies only ([Pimsleur study](https://comparelanguageapps.com/documentation/Pimsleur_EfficacyStudy2019.pdf)) | No rigorous outcome evidence | Borrow surface simplicity (audio-only prompt, pause, respond; no notes) but make no claims |

Not verified in this pass and therefore not evidence: Couch to 5K, Strava, Peloton, Noom, Headspace, Calm, Tiny Habits, StickK, Wordle, Chess.com, Brilliant, Khan Academy, Anki, Toastmasters, radio taiso, BBC Learning English, Deutsche Welle, NHK. Their shared pattern (a fixed small daily unit, a shared ritual, a progressive graded plan) is consistent with the habit and megastudy findings but not proven by them.

Ranked principles for a fully online product (A = direct RCT or meta-analysis, B = adjacent-domain RCT; simplicity 1 = trivial to build and explain):

| Rank | Principle | Evidence | Simplicity | In the session |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Recall-first atoms with a 1 to 6 day echo | A | 1 | Minutes 0 to 2 |
| 2 | Welcome-back reward, no streak reset; any 4 sessions a week counts as on track | A | 1 | On return after a gap |
| 3 | If-then anchor plan, set at onboarding and each Monday; reminders quote the learner's own plan | A | 1 | 15 seconds, weekly |
| 4 | Finish the week with a real task, AI-scored with "what to change next" | A to B | 2 | Day 5 session |
| 5 | Plan, check, reflect bookends | A | 1 | 20 seconds at start and end |
| 6 | Daily story shelf: one graded story, no quiz | A to B | 2 | Minutes 7 to 9 |
| 7 | Explicit chunks of the week | B | 1 | Every atom |
| 8 | Supporter sentence, automated | A to B | 1 | Once a week |
| 9 | Vote, see, revote, asynchronous and anonymised | B | 2 | 1 minute |
| 10 | Only-when-it-matters nudges | B | 1 | Notifications |
| 11 | Pod of five: a stable level-matched cohort sharing the weekly goal, exchanging one recorded voice note a week, no rankings, no chat (extends R23) | B to C | 2 | Weekly |

A model 10-minute session that applies them: 0:00 to 0:20 "Today I will..."; 0:20 to 2:30 recall echo (yesterday plus 3 to 6 days ago); 2:30 to 6:30 two to three new atoms on the week's chunks, speaking first; 6:30 to 7:30 vote, see, revote; 7:30 to 9:30 story shelf; 9:30 to 10:00 "I can / not yet". The weekly rhythm: goal and if-then plan on Monday, four or more sessions, the real task at the end of the week, the supporter sentence and the pod voice note.

What the evidence says not to do: punish missed days with streak resets; assume automated content pushes alone move outcomes (SMS alone had no effect in Botswana, so the app must make the learner produce, not consume); replace retrieval with re-exposure (review screens that show the answer before asking for it); personalise by learning styles (EEF rates it among the lowest-impact strands); trust intuition or pilot results about engagement features (expert forecasters failed in the megastudy, so run many cheap A/B tests at scale); launch self-paced content with no social layer (52% of MOOC registrants never start, [Reich and Ruipérez-Valiente 2019](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/The-MOOC-pivot-Reich-Ruip%C3%A9rez-Valiente/fdd37727342a0da25294ba57953ea918665a5a2d)).

Caveats: most of the strongest evidence comes from children or from US gyms and universities, so effects on adults learning English are inferred; implementation-intention, tutoring and extensive-reading effects all shrink in larger syntheses, so plan for modest real-world gains; the fully online decision gives up the single best-evidenced lever (a weekly human call), which makes the automated supporter sentence, the pod and the end-of-week real task the load-bearing substitutes and the first things to A/B test.

PRD changes applied from this section: session composer updated to the recall-first, bookended, story-shelf shape; R34 replaced with the automated supporter sentence; R33 reduced to an ES World referral link; vote-see-revote added as an item type; welcome-back reward and if-then weekly re-plan added to the UX requirements.
